# Configuring ChatBranch for your industry

ChatBranch ships neutral. It knows how to hold a conversation across six channels
and a phone line; it knows nothing about *your* business until you tell it.

This guide walks through configuring it for a real vertical end to end. Almost
none of it is code — it is settings, a flow, and a data mapping. **If you find
yourself editing Python to add a vertical, stop: something belongs in one of the
five layers below instead.**

---

## 1. The five layers (read this first)

Everything the AI knows arrives through one of five layers. They differ in who
owns them and how often they change. Getting a fact into the *right* layer is
the whole skill.

| Layer | Where it lives | Who edits it | Changes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hard rules** | `backend/aicore/persona.py` `HARD_RULES` — code, **not overridable** | nobody, by design | ~never |
| **Persona** | Settings → **AI Persona** (`crm.Setting` `aicore_persona`) | superadmin | rarely |
| **Skills** | Settings → **AI Skills** (`crm.AgentSkill`) | admin+ | occasionally |
| **Knowledge** | Settings → **Knowledge Base** (`crm.KnowledgeItem`) | admin+ | constantly |
| **Contact memory** | generated by `backend/aicore/memory.py` | the system | per conversation |

Read that table as a decision tree:

- *"Never claim to be human. Never harvest OTPs. Honour opt-out."* → **hard
  rules.** They are prepended to every prompt on every channel and every call,
  and no persona can edit them away. This is the floor that keeps a badly
  written persona safe.
- *"We're a bubble tea chain; be warm, use Manglish when the customer does,
  never promise a refund."* → **persona.** One block of prose. Rewritten when
  the brand voice changes, which is roughly never.
- *"When someone asks about bulk pricing, collect quantity and company name,
  then hand to sales."* → **a skill.** Skills are `use_when` + `instructions`
  pairs; `use_when` is the selector the model reads to decide whether the
  instructions apply. Add one per repeatable situation.
- *"We close at 7pm. Returns within 14 days, unopened. Raya sale runs 1–15 Aug.
  The warehouse flooded, deliveries are 2 days late until Friday."* →
  **knowledge.** Typed by an operator into a form, live immediately, on every
  channel.
- *"This customer prefers evenings and gets annoyed by follow-ups."* →
  **memory.** You do not write this; the summarizer does, after calls and idle
  chats, and it writes durable facts back onto the contact.

All five reach **every channel and both call transports** through the same two
injection seams (`aicore/assistant.py` for chat, the call dispatch metadata for
voice). You never wire a fact into WhatsApp and then again into email.

Two guardrails worth knowing before you start typing:

- **Knowledge retrieval is keyword + recency + priority, not embeddings.** That
  is deliberate: it is deterministic, debuggable and needs no vector store.
  Write titles, questions and tags the way customers actually phrase things, or
  the FAQ tier will not match.
- **The knowledge block is hard-capped at 1800 characters.** Urgent tiers
  (notices → events → facts) are assembled first and win the budget; FAQs and
  policies get whatever is left, top-4 by keyword match. A knowledge base of
  300 rambling rows makes the AI *worse*, not better.

## 2. Worked example: an e-commerce store

Meet **Northwind Trading**, an online homeware shop. They want the AI to
confirm orders, chase unpaid ones, answer delivery questions, and hand angry
customers to a human. Here is the whole configuration.

### 2.1 Decide the data shape first

Two model decisions drive everything downstream.

**`Record.type`** — what a conversation is *about*. Choices are fixed in the
model: `order`, `shipment`, `invoice`, `booking`, `ticket`, `custom`. Northwind
uses `order` for the purchase and `shipment` for the parcel.

**`Record.status`** — a free-text string, **your vocabulary**. The platform only
cares about one thing: the statuses in `flows/engine.py CLOSED_RECORD_STATUSES`
(`closed`, `done`, `resolved`, `cancelled`, `completed`) mean "nothing more to
do here", and `primary_record()` skips them when picking which record a flow run
and a conversation are about. Everything else is yours. Northwind picks:

```
awaiting_payment → paid → packed → shipped → delivered
                                 ↘ cancelled   (a closed status)
```

**`Record.due_date`** — whatever "when" means for that record type. For an order
it is the promised delivery date; for an invoice, the payment due date; for a
booking, the appointment. **Every time-based flow trigger is an offset from
this field**, so if you leave it null, time triggers will never fire.

**`Record.data`** — a free JSON bag for everything else: line items, addresses,
`tracking_url`, `link`. Two keys are special because the flow variable catalog
reads them: `data["tracking_url"]` fills `{{tracking_url}}`, and
`data["link"]` (or `data["url"]`) fills `{{link}}`.

**`Contact.attributes`** — a free JSON dict on the person, for whatever your
vertical needs that the fixed columns do not cover. Northwind stores
`{"loyalty_tier": "gold", "preferred_channel": "whatsapp", "first_order_at":
"2024-03-02"}`. Two notes:

- `attributes["preferred_channel"]` is read by the flow variable
  `{{preferred_channel}}` — that key is wired up.
- Attributes are **visible to the AI**: `aicore/assistant.py` puts the contact's
  status, tags, attributes and open records into the prompt. Do not put secrets
  there.

**`Contact.tags`** — a flat list (`["vip", "wholesale"]`). Unlike attributes,
tags are first-class in the flow builder: there is a `Contact has tag` trigger
and a `Contact tag` decision. Use tags for anything you want to branch on, and
attributes for anything you only want to *state*.

### 2.2 Write the persona

**Settings → AI Persona** (superadmin). You are replacing `DEFAULT_PERSONA`
wholesale — the hard rules stay regardless. Keep it under a page; a persona
competing with 1800 characters of knowledge for context is a persona that gets
ignored.

Northwind's:

> Personality: you are the customer care assistant for Northwind Trading, an
> online homeware shop. Warm, brief, practical. Sound like the friendly person
> behind the counter, not a support macro.
>
> Conversation rules:
> - Reply in 1–3 sentences, in the customer's language (English, Bahasa
>   Malaysia or Chinese) — follow whatever they switch to.
> - Lead with the answer, then the detail. One question at a time.
> - Always reference the order number when you have one.
> - Confirm every agreement in writing: the exact thing and the exact date.
>
> Order questions: answer from the order in front of you — its status, its
> promised date, its tracking link. If the promised date has passed, apologise
> once, give the current status, and say when we will update them next. Never
> invent a new delivery date.
>
> Payments: you may send a payment link. You may not offer discounts, waive
> shipping, or approve a refund — say a colleague will confirm.
>
> Escalation: hand over when someone asks for a human, is angry, reports a
> damaged or wrong item, or disputes a charge.

Notice what is *not* in there: opening hours, the returns window, the current
promo. Those are knowledge, and they change weekly.

### 2.3 Write the skills

**Settings → AI Skills** (admin+). Each skill is a name, a `use_when` selector,
and `instructions`. They render into the prompt as a `SKILLS` section on every
channel and every call.

> **Settings → AI Skills is the only writer of `crm.AgentSkill`.** The flow
> builder's skill sidebar is read-only — it used to own these rows and a stale
> browser tab could silently delete a skill an operator had just typed. Do not
> add a second writer.

Northwind's five:

| Skill | use_when | instructions (abridged) |
|---|---|---|
| Order status | The customer asks where their order is, or about delivery timing | State the order number, its current status in plain words, and the promised date. If a tracking link exists, send it. If the date has passed, apologise once and say when we will update. |
| Payment chase | An order is `awaiting_payment` and the customer replies | Confirm the item and total, offer the payment link, and ask if they would like to change or cancel instead. Never pressure. |
| Bulk enquiry | The customer asks about bulk, wholesale or trade pricing | Collect quantity, item, company name and delivery postcode, confirm you have noted it, then hand over to sales. Never quote a price. |
| Damaged item | The customer reports a damaged, wrong or missing item | Apologise plainly, ask for a photo and the order number, confirm we will replace or refund per policy, and hand over to a human. |
| Reschedule | The customer wants to change a delivery date or address | Confirm the current details, ask for the new ones, state we will confirm within one working day, and hand over. |

Skills are cheap. One per situation your team already has a canned answer for.

### 2.4 Fill the knowledge base

**Settings → Knowledge Base** (admin+). Five kinds, and choosing the right one
decides how the item is retrieved:

| Kind | Retrieval | Use for |
|---|---|---|
| **`notice`** | Tier 1 — *always* included while live | "Warehouse flooded, deliveries +2 days" |
| **`event`** | Tier 2 — always included while live | "Raya sale, 15% off, 1–15 Aug" |
| **`fact`** | Tier 3 — always included | Hours, address, support number, shipping fees |
| **`faq`** | Tier 4 — only on a keyword match | "Do you ship to East Malaysia?" |
| **`policy`** | Tier 4 — only on a keyword match | Returns, warranty, exchange rules |

Every item takes: `title`, `content`, `question` (FAQ only — the *asked* form),
`tags` (matching hints), `channels` (empty = all), `priority`, `active`, and a
`starts_at` / `ends_at` window.

**The date window is the feature that makes this maintainable.** A "sudden
notice" with `ends_at` set stops being mentioned the moment it expires — nobody
has to remember to delete it. Expired rows grey out in the UI rather than
disappearing, so they can be re-dated and reused next time the warehouse floods.

Northwind starts with about fifteen rows:

- **facts** — opening hours; the support WhatsApp number; free shipping over
  RM150; standard delivery 2–4 working days Peninsular, 5–7 East Malaysia.
- **policies** — 14-day return on unopened items, buyer pays return postage;
  damaged-on-arrival replaced free with a photo; no exchange on clearance.
- **FAQs** — "Do you ship to Sabah/Sarawak?", "Can I change my address after
  ordering?", "Do you do COD?", "How do I track my parcel?" — each with the
  question phrased the way customers actually type it, plus tags like
  `sabah, sarawak, east malaysia, shipping`.
- **events / notices** — added as they happen, with an end date.

Then use the **Preview context** panel (`POST /api/aicore/knowledge/preview`).
Type a real customer message, pick the channel, and it shows you the *exact*
`COMPANY KNOWLEDGE` block the AI will receive, plus how much of the 1800-char
budget it consumed. This turns "why did the bot say that?" from a code
investigation into a button. Use it every time you add a batch of rows.

### 2.5 Load contacts and records

Three ways in, in rising order of automation.

**By hand** — Contacts page → Add contact, or `POST /api/v1/contacts` (admin+).
Phone is the identity key within a tenant; a duplicate returns 409.

**By bulk sync** — `POST /api/v1/sync/records` with an `X-API-Key` issued by
`manage.py create_data_source`. This is the path for a nightly export from your
own system of record. One object per record:

```json
{
  "sync_timestamp": "2026-08-01T07:00:00Z",
  "records": [
    {
      "external_id": "ORD-10233",
      "type": "order",
      "title": "3× Ceramic mug",
      "status": "packed",
      "amount": 48.90,
      "currency": "MYR",
      "due_date": "2026-08-05",
      "data": {"tracking_url": "https://track/…", "items": [...]},
      "contact": {
        "external_id": "CUST-77",
        "name": "Siti Rahman",
        "phone": "+60123456789",
        "email": "siti@example.com",
        "language": "ms",
        "tags": ["vip"],
        "attributes": {"loyalty_tier": "gold"}
      }
    }
  ]
}
```

Rows land immutably in staging first, then a normalizer upserts them —
contact matched on `(tenant, external_id)` then `(tenant, phone)`, record on
`(tenant, type, external_id)`. A status or amount change books a `RecordEvent`,
so the timeline shows what moved. Each row is processed in its own savepoint, so
one bad row never poisons the batch; failures are visible via
`GET /api/v1/sync/batches/<id>` and replayable with `manage.py replay_batch`.

**By webhook** — connect Shopify (Settings → Integrations) and `orders/*`
webhooks create and update the same `Record` + `RecordEvent` rows automatically,
creating the contact when the payload carries a phone or email. Stripe's
`payment_intent.succeeded` books the payment; set
`metadata.record_external_id` on the PaymentIntent and it attaches to the
existing order instead of creating a standalone invoice record. Redelivery is
idempotent on both.

### 2.6 Connect channels

**Settings → Channels.** Northwind adds:

- a **WhatsApp Cloud** business number, role `reminder` — the compliant template
  cannon for order confirmations and delivery updates;
- two **Baileys** personal numbers, role `conversation` — the AI negotiation
  layer, inbound-first (read the ToS warning in the main README before doing
  this in any real deployment);
- an **email** mailbox for invoices and receipts;
- a **Telegram** bot, because a chunk of their wholesale buyers live there.

Account **roles** matter more than they look:

- `reminder` (default for Cloud) — templates, AI off by default.
- `conversation` (default for Baileys) — free text, AI on by default.

The per-chat Chatbot tick is stored **per sending account**, so a thread moving
between the business number and a personal number automatically uses the current
number's own setting. Templates never fail over to Baileys. Conversational sends
never fail over to the business number.

Then set the **operating knobs** in Settings (they are `crm.Setting` rows behind
`GET/PUT /api/whatsapp/routing-rules`):

- `ai_reply_mode` — `queued` (default) batches several customer messages into one
  coherent reply generated at send time, or `instant`.
- `business_redirect` — an inbound on the reminder number with AI off gets one
  auto-answer per 24h pointing at the conversation number.
- `cold_text_guard` — `block` (default) refuses free text into a thread that has
  never received an inbound on a conversation-role number. This is the single
  most important anti-ban setting if you use Baileys.
- Send pacing (Settings → AI Configuration): `queue_min_gap_s` / `queue_max_gap_s`.

### 2.7 Set the business variables

Five `crm.Setting` rows fill the `{{business_*}}` flow variables:
`business_name`, `agent_name`, `support_number`, `support_email`, `website`.
Set them once; every template, canned response and flow script picks them up.

### 2.8 Build the flows

**/flow-builder.** A workflow is triggers → actions → decisions → waits → end.
Toggle **Live** and `flows/engine.py` executes it on a ticker; the state per
contact lives in `crm.FlowRun` (inspect via `GET /api/flows/runs`).

Northwind's three:

**Order confirmation.** Trigger: `Record status is` = `paid`. Action: WhatsApp,
template mode, `order_confirmation` with `{{name}} / {{record_id}} /
{{due_date}}`. End.

**Delivery reminder.** Trigger: `Days before due date` = 1. Action: WhatsApp
free text — *"Hi {{first_name}}, {{record_title}} ({{record_id}}) is arriving
tomorrow. Track it here: {{tracking_url}}"*. Decision on `Contact's answer` →
a reschedule branch hands over to a human, everything else ends.

**Unpaid nudge.** A trigger node matches exactly one condition, so put the
timing on the trigger and the state on a decision: Trigger
`Days after due date (exactly)` = 2 → Decision on `Record status` =
`awaiting_payment` → Action: WhatsApp text plus the `Create Stripe payment link`
integration action (which writes the URL onto `record.data["link"]` and
`["payment_link"]`, so `{{link}}` renders it) → Wait 3 days → second nudge →
Handover.

**Variables available in any script or template** (the catalog is
`flows/engine.py variables_for()`, mirrored in `frontend/src/lib/flow/variables.ts`
— keep them in step if you extend it):

| Group | Keys |
|---|---|
| Contact | `name`, `first_name`, `phone`, `email`, `language`, `company` |
| Record | `record_id`, `record_type`, `record_status`, `record_title`, `amount`, `currency`, `due_date`, `days_until_due`, `days_overdue`, `tracking_url`, `link` |
| Business | `business_name`, `agent_name`, `support_number`, `support_email`, `website` |
| Memory | `best_contact_time`, `preferred_channel`, `last_contact_date` |
| Other | `situation` (the contact's notes field) |

An unknown `{{key}}` is left visible in the rendered text on purpose — a missing
mapping should be obvious, not silently blank.

Two engine behaviours to design around:

- **Time triggers read the primary record's `due_date`.** `Days after due
  date` is a `>=` match (it keeps matching); `Days after due date (exactly)`
  fires only on that day. Use the exact form for a ladder of nudges, or you will
  enrol the same contact into three steps at once.
- **Sends are queued, not inline.** Every WhatsApp action becomes a
  `crm.OutboundJob` drained with jitter and caps. `sent` on a call job means
  *dialled*, not completed. Inspect the queue at `GET /api/flows/queue`.

### 2.9 Connect integrations

**Settings → Integrations.** Northwind connects Shopify (orders in), Stripe
(payments in, payment links out) and Google Sheets (a daily "handed to human"
log via the `Append row to Sheet` action).

Remember what the main README says about maturity here: these mappings are
tested against fixtures built from documented payloads, never against a live
provider. Treat your first real connection as an integration test you are
running yourself, and watch `GET /api/flows/runs` while you do.

### 2.10 Replace the demo seed

`manage.py seed_demo` creates ten demo contacts with an e-commerce record shape,
two tenants, three brands, three users and five canned responses. It is
idempotent and safe to re-run.

For your own vertical, edit `DEMO_CONTACTS` and `DEFAULT_CANNED` in
`backend/crm/management/commands/seed_demo.py` — that is the one file in this
guide where editing Python is the right answer, because it *is* your demo data.
Keep the shape (a `contact` dict + a `record` dict per entry); change the names,
types, statuses and `due_in` offsets.

For a real deployment use **`manage.py seed_production`** instead: it creates
your real tenant + brand, the role set, safe runtime Settings and the canned
responses, with **none** of `seed_demo`'s fixtures (`--purge-demo` removes them
if they already landed), and can import a real contact book with
`--contacts path\to\contacts.json`. It ends with a readiness report of the
`.env` keys each subsystem still needs.

⚠ **`seed_production` does not solve the password problem for you.** The
superadmin password comes from `--admin-password`, else `SEED_ADMIN_PASSWORD`,
else a built-in default; admin and agent *always* get built-in defaults. Its
readiness report warns while any seeded user is still on one. **Rotate all
three before exposing the deployment** — `superadmin/super123`,
`admin/admin123`, `agent/agent123` are published in this template.

## 3. A second vertical, briefly: last-mile logistics

Same platform, different vocabulary. Nothing below is a code change.

| Decision | Value |
|---|---|
| `Record.type` | `shipment` |
| `Record.external_id` | the consignment number |
| `Record.status` | `picked_up → in_transit → out_for_delivery → delivered`, plus `failed_attempt`, `returned`, `cancelled` |
| `Record.due_date` | the delivery ETA |
| `Record.data` | `{"tracking_url", "address", "driver", "attempts": 2}` |
| `Contact.tags` | `["fragile", "signature_required", "business_address"]` |
| `Contact.attributes` | `{"preferred_window": "evening", "gate_code": "…"}` |

**Persona**: brisk, factual, logistics-desk tone. Never promise a time slot the
system has not confirmed. Never give the driver's phone number.

**Skills**: *Where is my parcel* · *Failed delivery, rebook* · *Change address
before dispatch* · *Damaged on arrival* · *Driver can't find the address*.

**Knowledge**: facts — depot hours, service areas, what happens after three
failed attempts. Policies — redelivery fees, holding period, proof-of-delivery
rules. FAQs — "Can I collect from the depot?", "Can my neighbour sign?".
Notices — "Flash floods in Klang, Zone 3 delayed" with an end date. That last
one is exactly the case the date window exists for.

**Flows**: `Record status is` = `out_for_delivery` → WhatsApp "arriving today,
here's your tracking link". `Record status is` = `failed_attempt` → a rebook
conversation, decision on the answer, hand over if they want a different
address. `Days after due date (exactly)` = 1 with status still `in_transit` →
proactive apology before they have to ask.

**Integrations**: Google Calendar for booked redelivery windows (`Find free
slot` then `Create calendar event`), Sheets for the exceptions log.

The pattern generalises the same way for a clinic (`booking`, appointment
reminders, reschedules), a B2B services firm (`invoice`, payment follow-ups) or
a support desk (`ticket`, status updates). What changes is the noun, the status
words, and the prose. What does not change is any Python.

## 4. Checklist

Before you call a vertical configured:

- [ ] `Record.type` and its **status vocabulary** written down, with the closed
      statuses matching `CLOSED_RECORD_STATUSES` where you mean "done".
- [ ] `due_date` populated on every record — otherwise no time trigger fires.
- [ ] Persona rewritten in Settings → AI Persona; hard rules left alone.
- [ ] One skill per repeatable situation your team already answers.
- [ ] Knowledge base seeded, and **Preview context** run against five real
      customer messages.
- [ ] Business variables set (`business_name` and friends).
- [ ] At least one channel connected — or deliberately left in sandbox.
- [ ] Flows built, tested against a seeded contact, and only then toggled Live.
- [ ] `seed_demo` data replaced, demo passwords rotated, `SECRET_KEY` set,
      `DEBUG=False`.
- [ ] The limitations section of the main README read by whoever signs off.
